The Magical City

Living history in every
corner of its streets

Founded in 1533 by Pedro de Heredia, Cartagena de Indias is a city that has preserved its colonial soul while embracing the modernity of the Colombian Caribbean.

Declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984, its architecture, people, and traditions make it a unique destination in the world.

Points of Interest

The Treasures of
the City

Every corner of Cartagena holds a story. Discover its most emblematic landmarks and let yourself be captivated by their beauty.

UNESCO Heritage

The Walled City

The historic center surrounded by imposing 16th-century colonial walls. Its cobblestone alleys, colorful mansions, and flowered balconies make this one of the most photographed places in Colombia.

Historic Fortress

San Felipe Castle

The largest fortress built by the Spanish in America. Its secret tunnels and bastions tell centuries of colonial Caribbean military history.

Natural Park

Rosario Islands

An archipelago of 27 islands with crystal clear waters and coral reefs. Snorkeling, diving, and paradisiacal beaches just 35 km from Cartagena.

Natural Paradise

Playa Blanca

One of the most beautiful beaches in the Colombian Caribbean. Fine white sand, palm trees, and warm waters on Barú Island.

Art and Culture

Getsemaní Neighborhood

The most authentic and bohemian neighborhood in Cartagena. Impressive murals, vibrant nightlife, and local gastronomy on every corner.

Historic Lookout

La Popa Convent

Located on the highest hill in the city, it offers unparalleled panoramic views of Cartagena and the bay. Founded in 1607 by Augustinian friars.

City Icon

Clock Tower

The main gateway to the walled city and the most recognizable symbol of Cartagena. A meeting point and entrance to the historic center since the 17th century.

Unique Experiences

Experiences you
won't forget

Sunset on the Walls

The spectacle of the sun sinking into the Caribbean seen from the colonial bastions is an experience that defines Cartagena.

Caribbean Gastronomy

Ceviche, egg arepa, seafood casserole, and coconut sweets. Cartagena's cuisine is a celebration of Caribbean flavors.

Music Festival

Home to the International Music Festival and the Film Festival, Cartagena vibrates with culture throughout the year.

Sailing the Bay

Explore Cartagena Bay by sailboat or catamaran and discover the city from the sea, like the pirates of old.

Practical Information

How to get there?

Air: Rafael Núñez International Airport (CTG) connects with Bogotá, Medellín, Miami, Panama, and more cities.

Land: From Barranquilla (2 hrs) or Medellín (12 hrs) by road. There are frequent buses from major cities.

Sea: Cartagena is a cruise port with regular international arrivals at the Convention Center.

Useful Tips

Currency: Colombian Peso (COP). ATMs are available throughout the city and shopping centers.

Local Transport: Taxis are the most comfortable option. You can also use platforms like InDriver or Uber.

Sun Protection: Use high SPF sunscreen. The Caribbean sun is intense all year round, especially between 10am and 4pm.

Hydration: Always carry water. Temperatures exceed 30°C and humidity can be high.

Safety: Stay in tourist and crowded areas, especially at night. The Historic Center and Bocagrande are very safe.

Language: Spanish. In tourist areas, you will find staff who speak English and other languages.
